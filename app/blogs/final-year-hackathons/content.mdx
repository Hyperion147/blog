# The final year that started feeling bigger than grades

There is something strange about final year hackathons.

They begin like any other competition: a form, a team name, a rough idea, and the promise that this time everyone will sleep early.

Then the night stretches, the code misbehaves, somebody orders chai, somebody else starts fixing a bug they did not create, and before you realize it the project becomes only half the story.

## Where the real memories came from

For me, the best part of those hackathons was not just building something under pressure.

It was watching friendships form in real time:

- the friend who could calm everyone down five minutes before a demo
- the one who somehow found the exact line of code causing trouble
- the person who kept morale alive with jokes when the whole UI broke
- the teammates who slowly stopped feeling like teammates and started feeling like people I would stay in touch with long after college

## A different kind of classroom

Hackathons taught me things that normal coursework rarely could.

They taught me how to explain half-formed ideas, how to trust other people with unfinished work, how to recover when the first version was bad, and how much easier difficult things become when the people around you are generous.

## The part I want to remember most

Years from now, I may forget the exact stack, the theme of a specific event, or what place we finished.

I probably will not forget:

- the rushed brainstorming on the way to the venue
- the snack wrappers around open laptops
- the relief after a successful demo
- the little celebrations after solving a silly bug
- the feeling of meeting people who made pressure feel lighter
